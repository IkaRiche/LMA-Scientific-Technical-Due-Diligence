# 01 — Research overview

## Origin and thesis

Lomidze Morphological Algebra (LMA) is an ongoing formal and experimental program initiated by Irakli Lomidze. It explores the idea that computation can be defined by **operative organization**, rather than only by a list of instructions and functions. Typed voids, boundaries, interfaces, composition order and history can be causal parts of a machine's permitted transitions.

The program has produced multiple *distinct* research and engineering implementations. One accepted result in one domain does not prove the same result in another. No universal dominance over neural networks, state machines, control theory, or established spatial models is asserted.

## Distinct tracks and their relationship

| Track | Research/engineering question | Current evidence tier |
|---|---|---|
| **LMA** | Can typed composition and morphic structure be made into explicit formal semantics? | Finite-model theorems; broader algebra not fully proven |
| **TMC** | Can bounded mission-conduct continuations depend on coupled structural and acquired-history components? | Scoped software science + MCU demonstrations; further validation required |
| **Morphic Vision** | Can a local perception channel be admitted and reproduced without covert global control? | Abstract/simulation-level tests only in referenced report |
| **Guardian Veil (original)** | Can heterogeneous autonomous nodes form protective spatial patterns via local interaction? | Digital-twin and bounded multinode simulation evidence |
| **Guardian Veil (relational)** | Can organism-owned needs and LMIO-like peer expression drive limited functional composition? | Scoped digital causal and higher-order evidence |
| **DRC/SRC** | Can a small independent boundary reject invalid commands and preserve bounded recovery? | Hardware-backed command-gate results |
| **Morphic Market / Morphic AI / LMCO** | Temporal structure; acquired causal behavior; organism-based cryptographic hypotheses | Separate research lines, not ready-made commercial products |

## Focused external review request

The immediate interest is in **decentralized organism-owned coordination**, **low-information peer communication and meeting behavior**, and **TMC's terminal mission-continuation computation**. These topics must be tested as separate claims. In particular, the accepted peer-message restriction does not prove universal coordinate-free rendezvous; synthetic civilian meeting studies do not qualify native Veil; and a successful TMC bench decision trace does not establish flight or end-to-end execution.

See the [focused claim screen](11_FOCUSED_AUTONOMY_REVIEW.md) and [challenge cards](12_REVIEWER_CHALLENGES.md).

## Why an investor might care

The potential opportunity is a **portfolio of differentiable technical mechanisms**, each of which can be subjected to a bounded industrial qualification program. Plausible customer segments include robotics integrators, embedded-system OEMs, safety-critical software vendors and specialist autonomous-platform manufacturers. These are **market hypotheses**, not validated purchase orders.

Defense/aerospace is a candidate domain because inspectability, isolation, reproducibility and constrained compute can be material procurement criteria. A single technology's relevance to defense does **not** prove suitability for any mission, threat or deployment.

## Next decision

The proposed outcome of this scientific review is **whether a deeper controlled examination is justified**, not an immediate allocation of equity, invention rights, or selection of a first product.
